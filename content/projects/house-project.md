## Lessons Learned

### What I'd Do Differently

**Have a coordinated planning meeting upfront.** If I could start over, I would insist on a proper sit-down meeting with the township, builder, and engineer to discuss in detail what a project like this would require—from subdivision through move-in. The lack of this coordination led to costly surprises mid-project that we might not have accepted if we'd known upfront.

### Unexpected Challenges

**The engineering phase was brutal.** What should have been straightforward turned into nearly 2 years (Dec 2023 - Sept 2025) of frustration. The engineering firm was unresponsive, extremely slow, and made repeated mistakes. Even the township was getting angry at them. This was by far the biggest bottleneck in the project.

**Township requirements blindsided us.** Mid-project, the township raised issues about:
- **Permeable land:** We weren't leaving enough permeable surface on my grandfather's property (which we subdivided from), so they required us to oversize our stormwater recharge system
- **Tree removal:** They wanted us to plant 60+ replacement trees. We negotiated down to planting 15 trees plus paying a fee-in-lieu for the rest

These weren't clearly communicated upfront and cost us significant time and money. If we'd known about these requirements and their costs in advance, we honestly might not have moved forward with the project or just paid to not plant any trees.

**Tree removal scope matters.** I hired an Amish crew for tree removal, but they only took the trunks—leaving stumps and all the tops/branches behind. It created a huge mess. Always clarify: are they taking just logs, or doing full cleanup?

### Best Decisions

**Choosing Clayton Homes.** The team at Clayton Homes of Paradise PA has been absolutely awesome. Now that the project is back in their hands, things are soaring forward.

**Living next to family.** We're building next to my grandfather and my parents. They're thrilled to see my daughters so much more often. That family connection makes all the struggle worthwhile.

**DIY site cleanup.** We did all the cleanup ourselves, saving a ton of money and having fun playing with heavy machinery.

**The floor plan.** Super happy with our layout, open and full of light. 2 Bathrooms! .

### Advice for Others Considering Manufactured Homes

1. **If possible, buy land that's already improved with utilities.** Subdividing and starting from scratch adds massive time, complexity, and cost.

2. **Be patient if subdividing.** The process is long and bureaucratic. Our engineering phase alone was nearly 2 years.

3. **RTFM - Read The F***ing Manual.** Don't rely solely on professionals. Read your township's ordinances yourself. Know the permeable surface requirements, stormwater rules, tree replacement policies, etc. We trusted the engineer to know these things, and they either didn't or didn't communicate them.

4. **Have that coordinated meeting.** Get township, builder, and engineer in the same room BEFORE you start. Discuss all requirements, timelines, and costs upfront. This gives you the choice to continue or walk away with full information.

5. **Vet your engineer carefully.** Check references, test their responsiveness, ask about their track record with your township. A bad engineer will bottleneck your entire project.

6. **Get multiple opinions on grading, stormwater, and foundation work.** Don't rely on a single expert.

7. **Clarify tree removal scope.** Make sure you know if contractors are taking just logs or doing full cleanup including stumps and debris.

8. **This is NOT a housing cheat code.** Yes, there are financial benefits to manufactured homes, but it's still expensive and complex. Don't assume it's the easy or cheap path to homeownership.

Despite all the challenges, we're excited about the outcome and grateful to be near family. The Clayton Homes team has been fantastic, and we're looking forward to moving in and making it home.
